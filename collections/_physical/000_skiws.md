# Introduction 

These skis were made as part of a course at my University ("Integrative Ski Building Workshop" at ETH). They are made from a wooden core, basalt fibre mesh and epoxy resin, using an autoclave to achieve good layer adhesion. Almost all the steps were done by hand, including bending the metal edge profile. Only the step of milling the wooden cores to size was outsourced and done by a local company. The bindings were also purchased from marker and installed by myself. The skis have successfully been ridden on slopes and in powder snow!

# Process 

Most of the fabrication process consisted of preparing the individual layers: a low-friction bottom material with the metal edge, a wooden core with plastic sides, multiple fibre mesh layers and finally, a top and bottom decorative element.

## Design
A basic design of the ski was made in CAD using Siemens NX. This served as the baseline to tune all the shapes as well as core thickness. 

## Metal Edge and Bottom Contact Layer

The bottom layer was cut by hand following a stencil designed in CAD. Using it as a guide, the metal edge could be bent to match it's shape - a tedious task done by hand with pliers.

## Wooden Core

Following standard lightweight construction principles, a wooden core will be sandwiched between two fibre layers. This achieves maximum stiffnes with minimal weight. The wooden core is made from balsa wood and has two plastic strips glued to each side to protect it from coming in contact with water later on. Additionally, the plastic strips protect against dents as they are quite a bit stronger than the wood. The glued part was machined to size by an external company.

## Mesh Layers

Multiple basalt fibre mesh layers were cut by hand. The number of layers, as well as their density and mesh direction determines the final ski stiffness and behaviour (together with the shape of course).

## Decorative Elements

To personalize the ski, some decorative elements were used. These could be placed on top of the assembly (to be the visible outside design of the ski) or, additionally, when using a transparent bottom layer, it is possible to put a decorative element directly behind it in the layup. I chose both options. Both elements were thin wooden layers to give the entire ski a retro wood-aesthetic.

## Gluing everything together

So far, all the individual layers were just prepared individually in parellel. In a final gluing step, all the layers were covered in epoxy and then stacked in the following order:

- top deocrative layer
- top fibre layer
- wooden core with plastic sides 
- bottom fibre layer
- bottom decorative layer
- low-friction bottom layer with metal edge

The entire layup was then left to cure under vacuum inside of an autoclave oven to achieve maximal bonding strength of the epoxy and push all the layers close together to avoid gaps.

## Cleanup and Bindings

Afterwards, a cleanup the sides and excess material had to be trimmed and cleaned up using a belt sander. However, the initial edge and bottom grind had to be done at an external company. The bindings were ordered from Marker and installed by myself.

# Result

I'm really happy with how the project turned out in general! Albeit being a bit stiff for a backcountry ski, they are perfectly rideable and even work on piste.
