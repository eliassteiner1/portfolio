# Abstract

Chronic pain is an emergent problem affecting a substantial amount of people across the globe. Among all types of chronic pain spinal pain is one of the most common. Often it is refractory to medical treatment and even re-operation is seldom successful. Spinal Cord Stimulation provides a viable solution in that case and can alleviate chronic pain even when other treatments fail.

While SCS is becoming more reputable it is not yet without flaws: especially during the implantation surgery complications can occur. The catheter implant is hard to manually control since it is inside of the human body and therefore physical trauma is not uncommon due to the catheter accidentally puncturing tissue. Additionally, surgeons receive incredibly high doses of radiation while performing the necessary fluoroscopy for this procedure. 
Therefore the aim of this project is to improve the SCS-procedure: a magnetically steered catheter combined with advancer-units enables the remote execution of this procedure and allows for precise control over the catheter, even once it is inside the epidural space. Finally a control algorithm will automatically manage the catheter steering and make the procedure simpler and more reliable. Through an already existing fluoroscope the controller can obtain visual feedback over the catheters position and operates in a closed-loop fashion.

With the help of specifically built silicone phantoms of the epidural space a prototype setup has been tested and further development possibilities have been layed out.
